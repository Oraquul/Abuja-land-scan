How the scores work

Every estate gets two scores. Use the switch above to rank by either.

  • Build in 1–3 years = price 25% + title 25% + infrastructure 25% + access 15% + price transparency 10%.
  • Hold 5+ years = price 30% + title 30% + growth catalysts 30% + price transparency 10%. Infrastructure and commute are left out, because a holder isn't moving in yet.
  • Price — half how cheap the estate is per sqm against the median of its own corridor, half how cheap it is across Lagos and Ogun overall (log scale, ₦4,000/sqm = 100, ₦400,000/sqm = 0). Survey, deed or development fees that are quoted are added in. A price below 40% of its corridor median has its price score capped at 70, because prices that low usually hide extra fees, a weaker location or a title problem. Undated or expired prices are capped at 50.
  • Title — the title the seller claims: Governor's consent or an individual Lagos C of O 90; Lagos state-scheme allocation (C of O) or Ogun C of O 85; estate global C of O 75; C of O with the issuer unclear 70; gazetted excision 60; excision with no gazette quoted 55; journalists' allocation letter 50; registered survey and deed 40 (45 with C of O 'in process'); receipt and survey 35; family receipt or deed only 30; none stated 25. None of these were verified at the Lagos Land Registry or the Ogun Bureau of Lands.
  • Infrastructure — how ready the estate is to build in now: residents already living there, tarred roads, fence, power, water and drainage score high; advertised future amenities score low.
  • Access — approximate off-peak drive to the nearest of three job hubs: Ikeja, Victoria Island/Lekki Phase 1, or the Lekki Free Trade Zone/Dangote refinery area. 10 minutes scores 100; each extra minute costs 1 point.
  • Growth catalysts — corridor-level judgement of what can push land values up over 5–10 years: the refinery, deep-sea port, Free Trade Zone and proposed Lekki airport; the Lagos–Calabar coastal highway; the Lekki–Epe and Lagos–Ibadan expressway upgrades and rail; the Epe food hub; Sagamu interchange industry. Mature, built-out areas score lower here because less upside is left.
  • Price transparency — recent, consistent prices with fees disclosed score high; promos, 'from' prices, expired offers and agents quoting different prices score low.
  • Grades — A 68+, B 60–67, C 50–59, D under 50. Estates with no usable price are mapped but not rated.
  • Before paying anywhere — in Lagos, get a lawyer to check the Official Gazette for the excision, run a search at the Land Registry (Alausa) on the estate's title, and check LASPPPA for setbacks and right-of-way (especially near the coastal highway and airport site). In Ogun, search at the Bureau of Lands, Abeokuta. Have your own surveyor confirm the plot sits inside the title, and get the full fee schedule (survey, deed, development levy, legal, agency 5%) in writing.

Prices shown are public asking prices, not a valuation or an offer. Titles are what sellers claim and have not been verified. Nothing here is legal, surveying, or investment advice.
